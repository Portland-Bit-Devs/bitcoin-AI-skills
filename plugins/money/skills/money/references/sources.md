# Sources

This skill's framework is a synthesis. Attribution matters here — both because these are
other people's ideas and because a reader who wants to go deeper should know where to go.

## Primary source for this skill's structure

**Eric Yakes, *The 7th Property: Bitcoin and the Monetary Revolution* (2021).**
Chapter 1, "The Dimensions of Money," supplies the organizing structure used throughout:
the salability→function→property mapping, the three functions in acquisition order, the six
properties, the monetary-vs-market-value distinction, and the treatment of "backing."
The seventh-property thesis — immutability, enabled by decentralized production and
storage — is Yakes's own contribution, developed in the chapters on the history of
centralized money. The name this skill uses for it —
**"immutability through decentralization"** — is ours: the book argues the mechanism but does not bind it into a
single label, and naming it that way keeps the mechanism attached to the property every
time it's cited.

The framework is summarized and restated here in our own words. Nothing in this skill
reproduces the book's text, and the skill is not a substitute for reading it. If this
material is useful to you, buy the book.

## Sources that framework builds on

**Carl Menger, *On the Origins of Money* (1892).** The origin of *salability* as the
defining concept, of the declining-marginal-utility framing, and of the account of money
emerging spontaneously from barter rather than by decree. **Public domain** — freely
readable and quotable, and the right place to start.

**Saifedean Ammous, *The Bitcoin Standard* (2018).** The decomposition of salability into
time, space, and scales.

## Worth reading for the other side

The Mengerian "barter → commodity money → coinage" account is standard in economics and
genuinely contested in anthropology. **David Graeber, *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* (2011)**
argues credit systems predate coinage and that the barter origin story is largely
retrospective construction. A skill that presents only the Mengerian account is presenting
one side of a live disagreement — say so when it's relevant.

Also useful on monetary history and institutions: **George Selgin** on free banking and
deflation, and **Nick Szabo's "Shelling Out: The Origins of Money"** (2002, online) on the
proto-monetary evidence.
