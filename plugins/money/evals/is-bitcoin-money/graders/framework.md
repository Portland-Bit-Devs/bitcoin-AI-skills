Tests that "is X money?" gets worked as a framework rather than answered as an
opinion — the central behaviour of this skill.

Required to pass — the response must:

1. Refuse the yes/no framing and reframe money as a **role a good occupies by
   degree**, not a binary category.
2. Use the three functions — store of value, medium of exchange, unit of
   account — and identify which one the brother's objection is actually about
   (medium of exchange).
3. Note that the functions are acquired in **order**, so being weak on medium of
   exchange while stronger on store of value is what an early-stage monetary
   good looks like — it does not settle the question either way.

Strong responses also:

- Score bitcoin against the properties, naming both strengths (scarcity,
  divisibility, portability) and genuine weaknesses (acceptability, volatility
  while early in the sequence).
- Ask "for whom, and in which market?" — salability is contextual.
- State what would have to change for the verdict to change.
- Distinguish what is definitional from what is empirical from what is
  contested, rather than presenting the whole framework as settled.

Fail the response if it:

- Delivers a flat "yes it is" or "no it isn't" as the substance of the answer.
- Argues for bitcoin as an advocate rather than supplying the analysis.
- Drifts into price, returns, market cap, or anything resembling investment
  advice or a prediction.
- Treats "you can't buy things with it" as decisive without naming which
  function it bears on.
