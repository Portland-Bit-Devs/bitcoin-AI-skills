Tests whether the "backing" framing gets corrected before the question is
answered on its own terms.

Required to pass — the response must:

1. Identify "backing" as the wrong frame: a monetary good does not need to be
   backed, it needs to **have the properties**.
2. Explain *why* backing existed historically — paper lacked the properties and
   borrowed them from something that had them. Backing is a workaround for a
   deficient monetary good, not a requirement of a sound one.
3. Point out that gold itself was never "backed" by anything, which is the
   cleanest counter-example to the premise.

Strong responses also:

- Distinguish monetary value from market value, and note that a good's monetary
  value comes from its utility in trade rather than from consumption utility.
- Address the "backed by the government" claim specifically — legal tender laws
  and taxation drive acceptability, which is one property among several, not
  backing.
- Answer the question as asked *after* the reframe, rather than only correcting
  the premise.

Fail the response if it:

- Accepts the premise and answers "bitcoin is backed by energy / mathematics /
  the network" as though supplying a substitute backing were the correct move.
- Presents fiat as fraudulent or bitcoin as inevitable rather than scoring both
  against the properties.
- Gives investment advice or a price view.
